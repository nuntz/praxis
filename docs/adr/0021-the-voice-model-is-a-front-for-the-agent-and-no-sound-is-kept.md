# The voice model is a front for the Agent and no sound is kept

Talking to the Agent is a Call: a live spoken exchange the person starts and ends by hand, inside one Context's Conversation. The model that listens and speaks is a front. It has no tools and is given nothing from the Journal, and it hands what the person asks to the one Agent, so each request is an ordinary Run on the same model, tools and rules as a typed Message. The person's Message is the text of what they said, the Agent's Message is what the Agent wrote, and the voice model speaks that in its own wording. Praxis keeps no sound anywhere. We decided this because a voice model that acted on the Journal itself would be a second Agent, with its own behaviour, its own evals and its own reading of the rules; because the Conversation is already where the person and the Agent work, so a spoken exchange kept apart would be a second one; and because a recording is the most sensitive copy there could be of something the Journal already holds as text once it is recorded.

## Considered Options

- **The voice model as the Agent**, calling the tool seam itself. Rejected: two Agents that act differently depending on whether the person typed or spoke, and a second set of evals.
- **Hold to talk with a written reply.** Rejected: the reply must be read, so it does not serve hands-busy use, which is the only reason voice is here.
- **A microphone on the Journal's composer.** Rejected: the Device's keyboard already dictates.
- **A Call as its own exchange**, apart from the Conversation. Rejected: a second Conversation per Context, with its own history, clearing and export rules.
- **Keeping the text of what was spoken as the Agent's Message.** Rejected: it is the text of a paraphrase and may hold mistakes, and what the Agent wrote is what the Agent stands behind.
- **Reading back before recording, or a spoken yes before a write.** Rejected: every capture becomes two turns, and the Agent already acts at once (ADR 0005). A mishearing is mended afterwards.
- **Keeping the audio**, to listen back or to check the text. Rejected: bytes to store, export, trash and protect, for something that is not the record.
- **Sound relayed through the Worker.** Rejected: the audio would pass through something Praxis runs for no gain.
- **The Context's object following the provider's side channel**, starting Runs from what it hears there. Rejected after a trial: the side channel sends every frame of sound, both ways, to whatever attaches, and nothing leaves it out. The Device's own channel to the provider carries the text and the handovers with no sound.
- **A Call that goes on in the background or on a locked phone.** Rejected: an open microphone nobody is watching, billed by the minute.

## Consequences

- A Call is the only voice in Praxis. It belongs to the Context it was started from until it ends; the Agent knows the other Contexts by name only and says to switch, which is done by hand.
- The voice model cannot be forced to hand everything over, so it may answer small talk by itself. It can say nothing of the Journal, since it knows nothing of it. What it says by itself, including "one moment", is not a Message and is not kept.
- The record may differ in wording from what was heard, and the voice may leave part of an answer unsaid, a change included. The Agent's Message is the record of what it did. In a Call the Agent is told it is being heard: it writes short answers, offers the rest, and always says what it changed.
- An Entry recorded in a Call uses the text of what the person said, with the person as its Author and no mark that it was spoken. A mishearing is fixed like any other Entry: by the person's edit, or by a Held Change, and the Versions keep the original.
- A Held Change made in a Call waits for a tap as always. A spoken yes does nothing, and the Agent says so.
- Talking over the Agent stops the speech and nothing else. The voice model is told to stop when the person speaks, and the speech stops as soon as it yields, which may take a moment: the provider offers no way to cut it off. A Run that has started finishes, its writes stand, and its Message is kept in full even if it was never heard. Undoing is a new request, done by ordinary means.
- The sound travels between the Device and the voice provider, and the Device carries the Call. The Context's object sets it up with the voice key, which never reaches a Device. The Device follows the text of what is said and the handovers on its own channel to the provider, sends each request to the object as it would a typed Message, marked as spoken, and passes the Agent's answer back to be spoken. The object follows nothing and holds no connection to the provider while a Call goes on.
- The object sets the Call up with the voice model and its instructions, which the Device cannot change, and lets the Device send the provider only the Agent's answer and the end of the Call. Where a request has no answer, its Message refused or its Run failed, the Device gives the voice what Praxis says of it instead, under the same handover, so a request is never met by silence. This keeps the instructions out of the Device's reach. It does not make the front something the provider enforces: the Device could still give the voice model text outside a handover, and only Praxis's own code on the Device keeps it from doing so.
- A handover carries no words and the text has no turns, so the person's Message holds everything they said since the last handover. It may open with a word the voice model already answered by itself, such as "hello", and may hold a sound written out, such as a sigh. Nothing said is left out.
- The Device ends a Call at the provider itself and waits to hear it closed, and says whether it did when it reports the end. The object attaches to the provider's side channel only to end a Call the Device has not: one it could not see close, one whose Device went silent, and one at the cap. It ends the Call at once and drops unread every sound it is sent until the provider says the session has closed, the few seconds of sound before it attached included. At the cap it first gives the voice one sentence, outside any handover, saying the cap is reached and the Call ends, and waits a few seconds for it to be said: the side channel is trusted, and the Device has no handover to say it under.
- The voice provider is a second provider with a second optional key, kept like the first (ADR 0017). Without it there is no way to start a Call and nothing looks broken; without the Agent's key there is no Call either.
- What is said in a Call, and what the Agent answers in it, reaches the voice provider and whatever that provider keeps for its own monitoring. Praxis never asks the provider to store a session. Setup says this where the key is given (ADR 0010).
- The voice model starts every Call blank and is never given earlier Messages. Continuity is the Agent's, which has the Conversation.
- The person's tap ends a Call, and so does leaving Praxis: the tab hidden, the screen locked or the network gone. It also ends after about two minutes of silence and when the cap is reached, saying so first. An ended Call is never resumed by itself.
- One Call at a time for the Deployment; starting another ends the first. The Deployment object keeps which Call is going and has the first one's object end it, once the new one is set up, so of two started at once the later stands. On the Device in the Call the Conversation's composer gives way to the Call's controls, end and mute. A Message typed elsewhere meanwhile is answered in writing only, and Runs in a Context still happen one after another.
- A Call's time is counted by the object's own clock, from setting the Call up until the Device reports its end, against the monthly cap at a price shipped with the code, on top of the Runs it starts. The Device sends a sign of life while the Call is on. When none comes for a short while, or the cap is reached, the object ends the Call at the provider itself, so nothing rests on the Device. A Call leaves a record with no words: when, how long, what it cost and why it ended. No Call starts at the cap.
- When a Run fails in a Call the voice says so and the usual Notice sits under the person's Message. When the voice provider fails or the connection drops, the Call ends with a Notice that is not a Message, and a Run already started still finishes. A refused voice key is a standing Notice.
- Nothing unprompted speaks: a Brief or a Review is heard only if the person asks for it in a Call, and Praxis never starts one. A Call is not a tool, so a Connection can neither start nor see one.
- In CI the voice provider is faked on the Device, with scripted text and handovers and no sound. Real Calls are checked by hand with the other evals (ADR 0015).
- A trial on 2026-11-14 bore this out: the Device's channel carries the text and the handovers without sound and takes the answer back, the object can attach to the side channel and end a Call it was not following, and the provider ends a session at two hours. Not tried: a phone, a Call of more than a minute or so, and a real Run.
